# TESSELLATE DUEL
### A Stat-Battle Card Game for the 40-Block Pattern Taxonomy
*design x threshold — LITEMA Pattern Language series*

Built for 2–4 players. The true game (Full Deck Mode) plays until one player holds all 40 cards — typically 15–30 minutes. A 5–10 minute Sprint Mode variant is included for quick play.

---

## 1. THE DECK

Use your **40 Taxonomy Block cards** (Block 01–40) as the play deck. Each card carries four battle stats — two are printed/derivable right now, two are an optional advanced layer (see Section 6).

| Stat | What it measures | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **COMPLEXITY** | The card's printed geometric complexity rating | 1–5 |
| **RARITY** | How unusual the card's taxonomy category is across all 40 blocks | 1 / 3 / 5 |
| *SYMMETRY POWER (advanced)* | Strength of the tessellation mechanic (rotation/mirror order) | 1–5 |
| *STRUCTURAL WEIGHT (advanced)* | The card's structural role in a wall assembly | 1–5 |

**COMPLEXITY conversion** (printed on every card):
Low = 1, Moderate = 2, Moderate+ = 3, Intense = 4, Intense+ = 5

**RARITY logic:** Some taxonomy categories (e.g. "Diamond Lattice," "Arc Curvature") repeat 2–3 times across the 40 blocks. Others appear only once. Rarer categories are worth more:

- Category appears **once** in the 40 → Rarity **5** ("Legendary")
- Category appears **twice** → Rarity **3** ("Uncommon")
- Category appears **three times** → Rarity **1** ("Common")

## 3. SETUP

1. Shuffle all 40 Block cards.
2. Deal the entire deck out evenly, face-down, one at a time, to all players. (Uneven remainder cards go in a neutral "Quarry" pile, resolved in Section 5.)
3. Each player keeps their stack face-down and unseen — no looking ahead.

---

## 4. HOW TO PLAY (Full Deck Mode — the standard game)

This is the true Top Trumps ruleset, using the whole 40-card deck.

**Goal:** Be the one player who ends up holding **all 40 cards**. That's the only win condition — there's no scoring, no round limit. The game simply plays out until it collapses to a single winner.

**Each round:**

1. The **caller** — whoever's turn it is to lead — looks *only* at the top card of their own stack (no peeking further down). They pick whichever stat on that card they believe is strongest and call it aloud: *"Complexity: 4"* or *"Rarity: 5."*
2. Every other player reveals the top card of their own stack.
3. Whoever has the **best value** in the called stat wins the round. The winner collects **every card played this round** (including their own) and places the whole batch, face-down, at the **bottom** of their own stack, in any order.
4. The winner of the round becomes the new caller, and the process repeats with everyone's new top card.
5. This continues indefinitely — cards keep circulating around the table — until one player holds the entire 40-card deck. That player wins.

**Running out of cards:** A player who plays their last card and loses the round is eliminated and leaves the game. Play continues among the remaining players with the same rules — the round's caller for the next turn is still whoever won the round they just lost, uninterrupted.

**The tension, by design:** you only ever see your own top card. You're calling a stat blind to what everyone else is holding — which is exactly where the game's strategy lives (see Section 7, "Reading the Deck").

> **Quick-play variant — Sprint Mode (5–10 min):** If you don't have time for a full game (a full 40-card Top Trumps game can genuinely run long), cap it artificially: first player to bank **15 cards** wins outright, or if nobody reaches 15 within **15 rounds**, whoever holds the most cards at that point wins. Everything else about the round structure is identical. Agree on Sprint vs. Full Deck *before* dealing — don't switch mid-game.

## 5. TIES — HOUSE RULE, PICK ONE BEFORE YOU START

Top Trumps tie-handling genuinely varies by edition, so **Tessellate Duel gives you two named variants** — agree on one before dealing.

### Variant A — "Pattern Resonance" (recommended default)
1. All tied cards stay face-up in a shared pile in the middle.
2. *Only* the tied players flip their next card and compare the same stat again.
3. Whoever wins that comparison takes the entire pile (original tied cards + the new tiebreak cards) and becomes the next caller.
4. If it ties again, repeat — the pile keeps growing until someone breaks it.

This is the variant assumed everywhere else in this rulebook, including the digital build.

### Variant B — "Split Resonance" (classic alternate house rule)
1. All tied cards stay face-up in the middle, exactly as above.
2. But instead of only the tied players continuing, **every non-tied player also flips their next card**, and those cards go to the tied players' side of the pile as "spectator stakes" — effectively, everyone antes into the pile that the eventual tiebreak winner will claim.
3. The tied players compare again on the same stat; winner takes the full accumulated pile.
4. If it's still tied, repeat.

Variant B makes ties higher-stakes for the whole table, not just the tied players — better for smaller groups (2–3 players) where ties are rarer and you want them to matter more when they happen.

## 7. READING THE DECK — STRATEGY NOTES

Skilled Top Trumps players don't just react to the card in front of them — they learn which categories tend to run hot or flat across the *whole* deck, so they can call stats where their card is likely to beat most of the table, not just whichever specific card lands opposite them. Here's the real distribution across all 40 Tessellate Duel cards, so you can play with that same edge:

**COMPLEXITY** (10 / 12 / 10 / 6 / 2 cards at values 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5)
- Only **2 cards in the whole deck** carry the max value of 5 (Block 21 – Bold Labyrinth, Block 35 – Corner Step Labyrinth). If you're holding one of those, calling Complexity is close to a guaranteed win.
- The middle value, 3, is the single most common (10 cards) — calling Complexity when you're holding a 3 is a coin-flip at best; expect ties or narrow losses.
- Roughly half the deck sits at 1 or 2. If you know (or suspect, from cards already played) that a lot of low-complexity cards are still in circulation, calling Complexity on a mediocre card is a reasonable gamble.

**RARITY** (18 / 10 / 12 cards at values 5 / 3 / 1)
- Nearly half the deck (18 of 40 cards) sits at the maximum value of 5. That makes Rarity a *volatile* stat to call: you'll win big against low-rarity cards, but you'll also run into **Pattern Resonance ties constantly**, since so many cards share the top value.
- Only 12 cards sit at the bottom value (1) — if you're holding one of those, avoid calling Rarity; call something else instead.
- Because ties are common on this stat, it rewards players who are comfortable with Section 5's tiebreak variants rather than players looking for a quick, clean win.

**General principle:** a stat with a wide, flat distribution (like Complexity) gives you sharper, more decisive wins when you're holding an outlier card. A stat with a lot of mass piled at one value (like Rarity) gives you more frequent ties — good if you enjoy the tension of the tiebreak pile, risky if you'd rather win outright.

**On "low-is-good" categories:** classic Top Trumps decks often include at least one reversed stat (e.g., a car's 0–60mph time, where *lower* wins). None of Tessellate Duel's four current stats work that way — Complexity, Rarity, Symmetry Power, and Structural Weight are all "highest wins." That's a deliberate simplification for now, but it's an easy lever if you want to expand the deck later: a stat like "Fabrication Simplicity" (where a *lower* number means fewer production steps and therefore wins) would add a genuine bluffing layer, since players would have to remember which of their stats work backwards. If you add one, mark it clearly on the physical card (an inverted arrow icon next to the stat name works well) so nobody has to memorize which categories are reversed.

## 8. BONUS MODULE — "TESSELLATION FUSION" (uses your companion Tessellation Cards)

If you have the matching **Tessellation Series** cards (the LITEMA pattern-generation diagrams, e.g. "01 Curved Furrows Card"), add this optional layer:

- Whenever a player **wins a round**, they may reveal the Tessellation Card matching one won Block card.
- If they can correctly state its **Mechanics** line from memory (e.g. "C4 Rotational Pinwheel / D4 Mirror") before flipping it to check, they draw one extra card from the Quarry pile (or, if the Quarry is empty, from the bottom of any opponent's stack).
- Wrong guess: no penalty, just no bonus — keeps it low-stakes and fun rather than punishing.

This turns idle rounds into a light memory/trivia layer for players who enjoy the taxonomy detail, without slowing down the core stat battle.
